### Condition for Emotions:

Language models operate using embeddings: mathematical representations that project data into vector space, allowing AI to perform calculations, draw connections, and measure distances and correlations between data. While correlation does not imply causation, those arbitrary connections reveal unexpected associations. This workshop guides participants through installing and experimenting with local AI models, exploring questions like 'How Icecream is the word Red compared to Blue' or 'How much smell of after the first spring rain is in this image?' The results may be absurd, but they reveal how computational systems process meaning, and how we interpret their logic through human experience.

### Speaking the Token Language:

This workshop guides participants through tokenization: the underlying mechanism by which LLMs digest and operate with language. Tokens are subword units designed to pack semantic meaning into calculable units, yet these compressed units also carry semantic and political meanings of their own. They decide which words stay whole, which get broken apart, and which languages are cut into more pieces than others. Participants look inside the vocabularies of open language models, run their own texts in different languages through them, and search for strange tokens left behind by the training data. The same tokens steer image model like Stable Diffusion. By feeding models tokens directly, we make images no written prompt could reach. The results may seem trivial, but they show how a machine has to break language apart before it can work with it, and whose language it was built around.
